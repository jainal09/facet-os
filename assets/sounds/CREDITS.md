# Sound credits

All five clips are **original work, released CC0** (public domain). They are not
sampled from any library, so there is nothing to attribute and nothing to clear.

They are generated by [`render.py`](render.py) — run `python3 render.py` to
rebuild them. Stdlib only, no dependencies.

## Why authored rather than sampled

The cube's speaker is a cheap micro-driver, measured on the actual hardware as
silent below ~500 Hz, faint at 500, and usably present from there to 8 kHz.
Sounds have to be *placed* in that band deliberately: fundamentals at
1.2–2.5 kHz, partials reaching ~6 kHz for shimmer, and a hard high-pass at
500 Hz because energy the driver cannot move comes back as distortion rather
than simply going missing.

Shopping for samples cannot hit a target that specific. Authoring them also
keeps a public repo free of licence questions, and makes every parameter
tweakable — "the bell is too bright" is a number, not a hunt for a new file.

## The set

| File | Event | Peak | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `tick.wav` | Rotation detent | −8 dBFS | Wooden knock: filtered noise contact plus a resonant body at 1.65 kHz |
| `start.wav` | Session begins | −13 dBFS | Two struck glass notes rising, D6→G6 |
| `pause.wav` | Laid flat | −14 dBFS | The same gesture falling |
| `resume.wav` | Stood back up | −15 dBFS | E6→A6, quicker and quieter |
| `done.wav` | Countdown finished | −11 dBFS | Struck bell, six inharmonic partials, second strike a fifth up |

Levels are deliberately unequal: the tick runs ~10 dB hotter than the cues
because a 75 ms transient needs more level than a 2 s bell to land with the same
weight. The device applies one master gain on top (default +12 dB), so the
balance between clips lives here, in the files.

Format is 16-bit mono 22050 Hz, matching the BSP's I2S default so the ESP32
never reconfigures the codec.
